# Phase 13: Climate Event Lab and selected tropical Pacific cases

Project date: 23 September 2026. Release 0.13.0, method `p13-climate-v1`, is deployed and verified on Vercel. P13.1-P13.5 and X23-X24 are complete for the supported historical cases. The separate P09.5 real unfamiliar-user pilot remains open.

## Supported investigation

Compare September-November 2013, 2015 and 2022 at the same native Pacific coordinates. The pinned official RONI v6 snapshot identifies these as neutral, El Nino and La Nina respectively. Users can inspect SON or one of its three constituent monthly fields, compare potential-temperature anomalies against the same calendar window of the 1991-2020 GODAS baseline, and examine exact depth profiles and signed selected-minus-reference differences.

The Indian Ocean section uses the same source product, calendar window and baseline. Its context does not establish that ENSO caused the displayed Indian Ocean conditions. Official IOD episode assessments and numerical DMI values keep their separate definitions. A documented episode is not silently inferred by thresholding the displayed seasonal mean.

The three Pacific seasons are genuine shared-workspace cases, with native 3D, depth-slice and section access plus original instrument inspection. Potential temperature is the supported Pacific model variable. Find structures supports threshold queries on that field, with actual 5-459 m depth limits and no direct observation association. The existing Indian Ocean HYCOM cases retain temperature, salinity and currents. The Pacific interface makes Compare, Expedition, Drift and Heat unavailable with a source-specific explanation. The climate views, Find structures and shared Ocean Explorer remain connected to the same native data. No unsupported current, heat-event or sampling-experiment path substitutes another field or dataset.

## Sources and calendar definitions

See [GODAS and Argo preparation](P13_FIELD_SOURCES.md), [official index snapshots](P13_INDEX_SOURCES.md) and [API/export contract](P13_API_CONTRACT.md). D044-D046 record the substantive choices.

Thirty-one GODAS annual source subsets cover SON in 1991-2020 plus 2022. These are reconstructed native OPeNDAP subsets, not downloads of entire original annual NetCDF files. The combined source geometry has three monthly means, 28 native depths from 5 to 459 m, 30 native latitudes within 5 degrees of the equator, and 240 native longitudes from 40.5 to 279.5 degrees east. Each Pacific case retains the original 140.5-279.5 degree longitude subset. Original source values are potential temperature in kelvin. Preparation masks invalid values, promotes source Float32 values to Float64 and subtracts 273.15. It assigns the CF quantity name from NOAA's explicit variable description while recording that the original COARDS file lacked that standard_name attribute.

GODAS timestamps denote the first day of the averaging month. Each interval is start-inclusive and end-exclusive: September runs from 1 September to 1 October, and SON runs from 1 September to 1 December. The app retains that support interval and does not present a monthly mean as an instantaneous measurement. All twelve combinations of three years and four calendar windows are checked. The equatorial section uses the native latitude nearest zero, 0.16612949967384338 degrees north, without interpolation to the equator. Longitudes remain monotonic on the source 0-360 degree axis, while display labels can use east/west notation. Selected profiles and values use native longitude/depth indices, without interpolation or extrapolation beyond the supplied 5-459 m range.

The baseline requires all 30 source years at each month/cell. Its 535,279 complete month/cells retain their calculated means; 69,521 incomplete month/cells stay missing. SON is the equal-weight mean of the three complete monthly means, in fixed order. It is not a day-weighted mean. Anomaly means event field minus matching baseline. A between-event difference uses the same native coordinates and calendar window. No result uses an unsupported field value to fill a gap.

Later source review located the official NOAA 1991-2020 climatology under `godas/Derived/`. Its metadata describes a Float32 product that permits output with at least 3% of inputs present. P13 retains its declared Float64, 30-of-30 reconstruction from actual annual fields. The earlier 404 from a guessed URL was not proof that no provider product existed. This corrected source explanation does not alter the chosen baseline or its hashes, and no numerical comparison with the provider climatology has been claimed. See [the archived metadata check](evidence/p13-official-climatology-metadata.json).

Seventeen acquired source documents support four separately named index definitions and three deployed event records. The official RONI v6 table is pinned separately from ONI v5 and ONI v6. The 2026 operational change is documented in the source review. The SON RONI values are -0.2, +2.0 and -1.1 degrees Celsius for 2013, 2015 and 2022. The qualifying warm/cold windows are checked against official provider classifications. Selecting September, October or November shows ASO, SON or OND index context respectively, with the actual three-month season name. It does not relabel a seasonal index as a monthly measurement, and it does not recompute RONI from the bounded GODAS fields.

CPC's specific downloaded ERSSTv6 DMI series declares a 1991-2020 baseline. The broader HTML page's different baseline wording is retained as a source metadata discrepancy. The published SON DMI values are -0.17, +0.39 and -0.48 degrees Celsius. BOM documents the 2015 positive IOD episode and 2022 negative episode. The 2015 SON value being below +0.4 does not erase that separate historical assessment or establish an identical time interval. No IOD episode label is invented for 2013.

Nine preselected original Argo profiles retain their original dates, coordinates, parameter-specific QC, raw/adjusted values and source hashes. September 2022 has 994 accepted temperature readings and no accepted salinity readings; joint core eligibility remains zero. The profile was not replaced to improve its appearance. Monthly potential temperature and instantaneous in-situ observations remain distinct, without a misleading direct residual or independent-validation claim.

## Demonstration and remaining limits

Open Climate Event Lab and compare 2015 with the 2013 neutral reference. Inspect the fixed SON event labels and their sources. Compare the two potential-temperature anomaly sections on a common scale. Select the same native longitude and depth in both years, then inspect the profiles and exact selected-minus-reference value. Switch to October to inspect actual monthly support and the separately labelled SON index context. Choose 2022 to examine the La Nina case. Expand Indian Ocean context and distinguish the DMI value from the BOM historical episode label.

Open the selected Pacific case in Ocean Explorer, inspect potential temperature and a genuine Argo profile, then return to the climate comparison. Save the applied settings, replay them and inspect the exported sections, baselines, profiles and index definitions. These actions pass the final public browser and exact replay/export checks.

Three selected seasons do not establish equal coverage of every ENSO or IOD event. No event-strength control produces a new forecast, and regional historical differences do not prove causality. P09.5 real unfamiliar-user testing, physical-device coverage and the project's existing external validation and hosting limits remain open. Next implementation after acceptance is P14 feature evolution and observation blackout, with Ultra recommended, only on separate user authorization.
