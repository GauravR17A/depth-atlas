NOAA OISST v2.1 daily sea-surface temperature
Huang et al. (2020), NOAA 0.25-degree Daily Optimum Interpolation Sea Surface
Temperature (OISST), Version 2.1, NOAA National Centers for Environmental
Information. DOI: https://doi.org/10.25921/RE9P-PT57
Data provided by NOAA PSL, Boulder, Colorado, USA, from https://psl.noaa.gov.
https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/optimum-interpolation-sst
https://psl.noaa.gov/data/gridded/data.noaa.oisst.v2.highres.html
https://www.noaa.gov/disclaimer
Retain source attribution. This is a blended/interpolated SST analysis,
not direct observations at every grid cell or an institutional endorsement.
NOAA documents that the pre-2016 SST values are unchanged from OISST v2;
original legacy metadata is preserved where present in the annual archive.

Surface marine-heatwave method:
Hobday et al. (2016), A hierarchical approach to defining marine heatwaves,
Progress in Oceanography. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pocean.2015.12.014
The application implements its declared event rules independently.
Eric C. J. Oliver's marineHeatWaves code is used separately for offline
reference checks under its own licence; it is not bundled in this website.
https://github.com/ecjoliver/marineHeatWaves

Mixed-layer threshold method family:
de Boyer Montegut et al. (2004), Mixed layer depth over the global ocean,
Journal of Geophysical Research. https://doi.org/10.1029/2004JC002378
The selected reference level and criteria are stated in the application.


Phase 13 historical climate sources
NOAA Global Ocean Data Assimilation System (GODAS), monthly potential
temperature, distributed by NOAA Physical Sciences Laboratory, Boulder,
Colorado, USA. https://psl.noaa.gov/data/gridded/data.godas.html
Native subset values and source masks are retained. Kelvin is converted to
degrees Celsius. The application derives a fixed 1991-2020 monthly baseline;
that baseline is not a separate provider anomaly product. GODAS assimilates
observations and is not an independent forecast validation dataset.

NOAA Climate Prediction Center: pinned Relative Oceanic Nino Index (RONI),
ERSSTv6, historical ONI v5/v6 references, and the ERSSTv6 seasonal Dipole
Mode Index (DMI). The specific source definitions, snapshots, hashes and
calendar windows accompany the app and exported investigations.
https://www.cpc.ncep.noaa.gov/products/analysis_monitoring/enso/roni/
https://www.cpc.ncep.noaa.gov/products/international/ocean_monitoring/IODMI/DMI_season.html

Australian Bureau of Meteorology annual climate statements for 2015 and
2022 provide separately attributed historical IOD episode context. These
are not classifications inferred by thresholding the app's SON DMI value.
https://www.bom.gov.au/climate/current/annual/aus/2015/
https://www.bom.gov.au/climate/current/annual/aus/2022/

Pacific Argo data use the International Argo Program/GDAC attribution
already retained above. Each original file keeps its contributing DAC,
source URL, retrieval identity and quality flags. No institutional
endorsement is implied by the use of these public sources.


Phase 15 wider sources (26 September 2026)
NOAA NCEP GODAS: monthly potential temperature and source salinity, September and October 2022, native levels 5-459 m. Data provided by NOAA PSL, Boulder, Colorado, USA. https://psl.noaa.gov/data/gridded/data.godas.html
GOBAI-O2 product v2.2, NCEI archive release 4.4, monthly oxygen and provider uncertainty for September and October 2022. Sharp, J. D., Fassbender, A. J., Carter, B. R., Johnson, G. C., Schultz, C., and Dunne, J. P. doi:10.25921/z72m-yz67. Original product licence: CC0 1.0 Universal. Original GOBAI-O2-v2.2-license.txt is retained in the source pack. https://www.nodc.noaa.gov/archive/arc0207/0259304/4.4/data/0-data/
GOBAI method reference: Sharp et al. (2023), https://doi.org/10.5194/essd-15-4481-2023. The paper describes v2.1; this application explicitly pins the v2.2 distributed product. The app selects original coordinates and retains source uncertainty without rerunning the external model.
