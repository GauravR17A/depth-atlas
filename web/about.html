Why we are building it

A surface map tells only part of the ocean's story. Scientists also need to inspect what changes with depth, compare model calculations with measurements and keep track of where the evidence came from.

Depth Atlas brings these activities into one browser workspace for scientific exploration and public outreach, with a guide for visitors who are new to the subject.

Three ways to investigate

  1. 01

    See below the surface

    Turn the 3D view, open a cutaway and inspect depth, time, temperature, salinity or currents.

  2. 02

    Check the evidence

    Open a sensor profile, inspect quality flags and compare compatible measurements with the model.

  3. 03

    Try a scientific question

    Explore virtual particle paths, heat and climate comparisons, sampling plans and changes in ocean structures.

What you can explore today

Selected Bay of Bengal and Arabian Sea cases contain January 2024 model fields. An additional Bay of Bengal case covers 28 to 30 March 2024. The tropical Pacific examples cover selected 2013, 2015 and 2022 seasons. Wider source regions use supported September and October 2022 products. These are historical datasets, not live 2026 conditions or equal coverage of every ocean.

The app distinguishes observations, model fields, derived results and simulations. Saves and exports keep their sources and settings. Basic and In-depth tutorials demonstrate the actual tools, and the explanation panel uses current results without an external language-model connection.
